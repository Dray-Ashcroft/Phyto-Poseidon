Positive hydrotropism

Roots grow toward wetter soil. The root tip senses a difference in water potential (ψ) across its two flanks. In Arabidopsis, both sensing and the growth response happen in the elongation zone: the ABA-signalling kinase SnRK2.2 and the hydrotropism-specific protein MIZ1 act in cortex cells there, and the cortex on the drier flank elongates more, so the root curves toward the water (Dietrich et al., 2017).

Cytokinin also builds up on the drier, lower-ψ side of the root tip, raising cell division there (Chang et al., 2019). Hydrotropism needs MIZ1 but works independently of the other tropisms (Kobayashi et al., 2007).

How the model is set

Lag phase: curvature starts within about 30 min of a moisture gradient (Takahashi et al., 2003).

Gradient: roots curve away from agar holding 0.5–1.5 MPa of sorbitol and reach 80–100° within 24 h (Takahashi et al., 2002). Here the response grows with Δψ up to 1 MPa.

Gravity: a moisture gradient breaks down the starch-filled amyloplasts in the root cap columella. Under a strong gradient they fall below 60% of normal within 30 min and to about 15% by 4 h (Takahashi et al., 2003). The model follows this time course, scaled by the gradient, and weakens the gravitropic pull to match. Regrowth after the gradient is removed is slower (6 h, illustrative). Gravitropic correction follows the sine law, as in Phyto Helios.

Checks against the data: at Δψ 0.6 MPa the root reaches about 79° by 24 h, and at 1.0 MPa about 88°, within the 80–100° reported for sorbitol split agar. At 1.0 MPa the root turns up to about 13° per hour, close to the 4–8 h needed to pass 90° under a steep humidity gradient.

Cortex growth: a root of diameter d that turns at ω (radians per hour) needs one flank to elongate ω·d faster than the other. Each flank therefore differs from the mean rate v by ±ω·d/2v. With d = 120 µm and v = 0.2 mm h⁻¹ (an Arabidopsis primary root) this is about ±4% at the peak, which the Cortex growth chart shows.

Magnifier: the lens shows the “Root Structure” model, a cut-away root tip, bent to the model's tip angle. The bend is a true arc in the elongation zone, so the outer, drier flank is longer than the inner one, while the meristem and root cap stay straight and turn with the tip. Teal marks MIZ1/SnRK2.2 activity in the elongation-zone cortex on the drier flank, and the root cap shows its remaining amyloplasts.

The 120 h (5-day) timeline compresses root growth for display. The timing follows the figures above; the root system's size and the response constants are illustrative.
